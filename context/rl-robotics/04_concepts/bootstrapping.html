Bootstrapping

Updating an estimate towards a target that contains the estimate being learned: the property that lets a single transition carry information, and one of the three ingredients whose combination makes approximate value learning diverge.

Where it sits

  • Unifying diagram cell: cross-cutting — it is the rule that separates the bottom two rows of the diagram
  • Failure mode addressed: none; it introduces the first ingredient of failure mode 1 (Week 7)
  • Course first appearance: Week 5

Core idea

A Monte Carlo target is assembled entirely from things the plant produced. A TD target replaces the tail of that sum with the learner’s own current estimate, which is what allows an update after one transition and what allows a continuing task to be learned at all. The same self-reference turns the estimator into a closed loop, so it needs a stability argument that an averaging estimator does not.

Formal definition

Given a fixed policy π, compare the two targets for V(xt​):
no bootstrappingGt​=i≥0∑​γirt+i+1​​​,bootstrappedrt+1​+γV(xt+1​)​​.
The first is an unbiased sample of Vπ(xt​) whatever the table holds. The second is unbiased for (TπV)(xt​) at the current V, and biased for Vπ(xt​) by
h(V)=TπV−V=(I−γPπ)(Vπ−V).
The depth of the handover is a continuous knob, the n-step return and the λ-return, so bootstrapping is a quantity rather than a switch. See Bias-variance tradeoff in RL and Eligibility traces.

The dividing line. The course applies this test three times. A quantity bootstraps when it predicts its own next prediction, so a fixed-point problem is being solved. A Monte-Carlo baseline b(x) fitted to observed returns involves no Bellman object and does not bootstrap; a critic fitted to r+γV^(x′) does. This is why REINFORCE with a baseline stays in the policy row of the Unifying diagram while PPO, whose value network is fitted on bootstrapped targets, sits in the actor-critic row despite its policy-gradient ancestry.

What bootstrapping is not responsible for (Week 10). The habit of reading “Monte Carlo is unbiased, bootstrapping is biased” as a property of the bootstrap survives contact with a great deal of material and is wrong. Writing e=Vπ−Vw​ for the error of the value estimate consulted, the bias a bootstrapped target injects into an advantage estimate is e(xt​)−γE[e(xt+1​)∣xt​,ut​], which is zero for every γ and every trajectory when e≡0. Week 10 measures this directly: with the exact Vπ supplied as Critic, the squared bias of the advantage estimate is flat across the whole λ dial from the one-step target to the full return. What the bootstrap does is make an estimator inherit the error of the predictor it consults, and λ decides how much of it. The distinction is load-bearing for Week 12, where the bias of offline methods comes from data that never covered the states the value function is asked about, which is a statement about e and not about the presence of a bootstrap.

What it buys, measured. Week 6 runs both sides of that line on one plant with one table size. Q-learning moves information through the value function one transition at a time and reaches 97% of the optimum after 7.5×105 transitions; Policy gradient (REINFORCE) assembles its target from complete observed returns and is still 8.7% short after 6.5×106. The efficiency has a matching cost in Week 7, where bootstrapping is the first of the three ingredients that make approximate value learning diverge.

Connection to classical control

An averaging estimator is open-loop: it collects a record, forms a statistic, and never consumes its own output while the record is being collected. A bootstrapped estimator is closed-loop, since its current output enters its own next measurement, which is exactly why Week 5 has to produce a mean flow and bound its spectrum before claiming convergence. The trade has the shape of every open-loop against closed-loop comparison in control, with correctness easy to certify on one side and responsiveness on the other.

Key limitation

Bootstrapping is biased at every finite time, and the bias is towards whatever the table currently holds. Under Function approximation in RL the damage is worse than bias: the backup becomes a projection composed with the Bellman operator, and a projection composed with a contraction need not be a contraction in any norm the algorithm respects, so the mean flow can acquire an eigenvalue in the right half plane. The uncomfortable symmetry of Week 5 is that the property bought the method its efficiency and will be named in Week 7 as one of the three causes of divergence.
