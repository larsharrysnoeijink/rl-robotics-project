Baseline (variance reduction)

Any function b(x) of the state alone, subtracted from the return in a policy-gradient update, which leaves the expected gradient exactly where it was and centres the multiplier so that actions better than the state’s typical outcome are pushed up.

Where it sits

  • Unifying diagram cell: a policy × no dynamics model. A baseline holds a value estimate and keeps the method in the policy row, because the row rule asks whether that estimate solves a fixed-point equation and this one is fitted by regression on observed returns.
  • Bellman object approximated: none. bw​ predicts no prediction of its own, which is the distinction Week 10 gives up when the baseline becomes a critic.
  • Failure mode addressed: 2, high variance in policy optimisation. It reduces the variance and does not remove it.
  • Course first appearance: Week 6 as the gap the hand trace exposes, formalized in Week 9

Core idea

On a plant with a negative stage cost every return is negative, so every sampled action is pushed down, including the best one available. Learning then proceeds only because better actions are pushed down less, which means the comparison between two actions happens across episodes instead of inside one. Subtracting the state’s typical return restores the comparison to where it belongs, and it costs nothing, because the score of a policy has zero mean at every state and therefore annihilates anything that is known once the state is known.

Formal definition

A baseline is any b:X→R. The estimator
g^​(b)=∑t​γt∇θ​logπθ​(ut​∣xt​)(Gt​−b(xt​))
satisfies E[g^​(b)]=∇θ​J for every such b, because the term it adds at a state is −b(x)Eu∼πθ​​[∇θ​logπθ​(u∣x)]=−b(x)∇θ​1=0. The condition has one form that survives contact with practice. The quantity subtracted may depend on the state and may depend on θ, and it may not depend on the sampled action. Writing st​=γt∇θ​logπθ​(ut​∣xt​), the mean of st​(Gt​−b) is free of b, so minimising the variance is minimising the second moment and gives
b⋆=E[∥st​∥2]E[∥st​∥2Gt​]​,
a return average weighted by how hard each step pushes on the parameters. Second moments of the score are not estimated in practice, so the standard choice drops the weighting and takes the plain conditional mean b(x)=Vπ(x)=E[Gt​∣xt​=x]. Two properties follow. A conditional mean is the solution of a least-squares problem, so bw​ is fitted by regressing on returns the episodes already produced, with no new data. And the centred multiplier Gt​−Vπ(xt​) estimates the Advantage function Aπ(xt​,ut​)=Qπ(xt​,ut​)−Vπ(xt​).

Anchor numbers

Measured at a frozen policy on the 961-cell swing-up with five torques, reported as Var(g^​)/∥Eg^​∥2 for one episode. The estimator ladder runs 4.3×103 with the whole trajectory return on every step, 2.0×103 for the reward-to-go form, and 68 once returns are centred by the exact time-indexed value. Sweeping a constant b through the reward-to-go estimator gives 7.4×102 at b=E[G]=−326 and 3.0×102 at the optimal constant b⋆=−216, worth 6.8× against b=0, while a state-dependent b(x)=Vπ(x) reaches 1.8×102, worth 11.3×. The best constant is not the mean return, since b⋆ weights returns by ∥st​∥2 and the steps where the score is large are the steps where the policy is most committed. Over a full run of 60000 episodes at α=3×10−5 with ten seeds, the baseline reaches plain REINFORCE’s final policy value using 5.4× fewer transitions, 1.2 million against 6.5 million, and the inter-seed spread at the end of the budget falls from 3.5 to 0.4 in policy value. Variance removed from an estimator shows up as agreement between runs that differ only in their seed.

Connection to classical control

Centring a measurement by its expected value before feeding it to an estimator is the standard move of removing a known bias term so that the residual carries the information. The closer analogue is the control variate of Monte-Carlo simulation, where a quantity with known mean and positive correlation with the estimand is subtracted to shrink the spread without touching the target. The choice b=Vπ is the control variate that a controls audience would guess, since it is the deterministic part of the return predicted by the state, and what survives the subtraction is the part attributable to the action taken and the tail that followed it.

Key limitation

Centring removes the offset between a state’s typical return and zero. It leaves untouched the spread of Gt​ around Vπ(xt​), which comes from the transitions and the later actions of the same episode, and 68 is the floor that measurement reaches even with an exact time-indexed value function. A relative variance of 68 still means dozens of episodes per update. Reducing the residual requires replacing the observed tail by a prediction and accepting bias, which is what Week 10 does by changing the target in the regression step from the observed return to rt+1​+γbw​(xt+1​). That single substitution turns bw​ into a Critic and moves the method into the bottom row of the Unifying diagram. One further caveat belongs to the algorithm rather than to the identity. Fitting bw​ on the very episode in which it is used couples it to Gt​, so the estimator is exactly unbiased only for a baseline built from earlier episodes. The correlation is small and shrinks with the regression step size, and it should not be heard inside the word unbiased.
